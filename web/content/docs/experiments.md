# Recorded experiments

Six full-brain simulations ship with the site so anyone can explore without installing anything. Each ran for 1 second of simulated time per trial. They are regenerated by `python scripts/export_web_data.py`.

| Experiment | Stimulus | Rate | Trials | Neurons that fired | Spikes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sugar taste → feeding | 20 sugar gustatory receptor neurons | 200 Hz | 5 | 436 | 100,697 |
| Looming threat → escape | 210 LPLC2 looming detectors | 150 Hz | 3 | 1,107 | 199,771 |
| Forward walking | 2 DNp09 descending neurons | 200 Hz | 3 | 309 | 16,623 |
| Giant fiber escape | 2 DNp01 giant fibers | 200 Hz | 3 | 65 | 3,669 |
| Moonwalker | 4 MDN descending neurons | 200 Hz | 3 | 29 | 4,416 |
| PPL1 dopamine | 16 PPL1 dopamine neurons | 100 Hz | 3 | 10,718 | 1,561,223 |

## Sugar taste → feeding

The model's headline result from Shiu et al. The sugar neurons in the subesophageal zone recruit a small, local circuit that ends in **MN9**, the motor neuron that extends the proboscis to feed (~115 Hz). Also active: MN10/MNx01 motor neurons and several DNge descending neurons. Only about 0.3% of the brain responds, so the signal stays focused.

## Looming threat → escape

LPLC2 neurons in the optic lobe detect objects approaching on a collision course. Stimulating all 210 of them recruits 1,107 neurons, mostly in the optic lobe and the posterior slope, including descending neurons associated with escape. This is a sensory-to-motor path spanning half the brain.

## Forward walking, giant fiber, moonwalker

These start at the *output* end. Stimulating descending neurons shows who else they talk to inside the brain: DNp09 recruits other walking-related descending neurons (DNa06, DNg98…), while the giant fiber and MDN drive small, specific partner sets. Most of their real targets live in the ventral nerve cord, which this model doesn't include, so their brain-side activity is small.

## PPL1 dopamine

The dramatic one. Just 16 dopamine neurons, which in real flies signal punishment to the mushroom body (the learning centre), recruit **10,718 neurons**. Activity comes in waves around 300, 450 and 550 ms as recurrent mushroom-body loops (Kenyon cells, MBONs, DPM, APL) ignite. In a real fly, dopamine *modulates* plasticity rather than simply exciting its targets. The model treats it as excitatory, so this run shows the connectivity reach of these cells, not their true physiological effect. That's exactly why learning rules are next on the roadmap.

## Running your own

See [Install and run](/docs/getting-started/). Good first experiments:

- `stim sugar run` then `stim sugar silence CB0616 run`, then `compare`
- `stim looming silence DNp01 run`: does escape still reach other descending neurons?
- `stim PAM05 run`: reward dopamine instead of punishment
